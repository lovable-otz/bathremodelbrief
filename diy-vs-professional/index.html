DIY vs professional bathroom remodeling

Reviewed 2026-08-18Every figure cited to a named source

Some of a bathroom remodel is genuinely within reach of a competent DIYer, and some of it is where a mistake stays invisible until it causes water damage years later. This section is about where that line sits, and what is actually at stake on each side of it.

This is a real question with a real answer, and the answer is not "always hire someone." It is also not "anyone can retile a shower." The useful version is specific: which parts of the job, with which consequences if they go wrong.

The parts of the job, separated

  • Demolition — physical work, and reasonably forgiving if done carefully.
  • Plumbing and electrical rough-in — in most jurisdictions this legally requires a licensed professional and a permit, not just skill. Check your local code before assuming otherwise.
  • Waterproofing — the step where a mistake is both easy to make and the most expensive to discover later, because it fails invisibly, behind tile, as rot or a leak into the floor below.
  • Tile setting — genuinely learnable, and where a motivated DIYer can save real money, though it rewards practice on a low-stakes area first.
  • Fixture installation and finishing — trim, hardware, painting. The most forgiving part.

The two steps worth being honest about

Plumbing and electrical work is often legally restricted, not just technically hard - many jurisdictions require a licensed plumber or electrician for anything beyond fixture swaps, and an uninspected illegal rough-in can be a real problem at resale, not only a safety one. Check your local requirements before assuming you can do this part yourself.

Waterproofing behind tile is the step where amateur mistakes hide the longest. A shower pan or wall that isn't correctly waterproofed can look perfect for months before the drywall behind it starts to fail. This is the single highest-value place to spend on a professional if you are doing some of the rest yourself.

The honest trade-off

What you save on the tile-setting and finishing work is real. What you take on with plumbing, electrical and waterproofing is risk that does not show up on the day you do the work - it shows up later, as a failed inspection, an insurance question, or a rot problem that costs more to fix than the professional labor would have.

Common questions

Can I do my own plumbing if I'm confident I know what I'm doing?

Check your local jurisdiction first - many require a licensed plumber for anything beyond fixture swaps, regardless of your own skill, and unpermitted work can be a real issue if you ever sell the house.

Which single part should I not do myself?

If you take one thing from this section: waterproofing behind tile is the step where a mistake is both easy to make and the most expensive to find out about later.

Can I do some of it and have a contractor do the rest?

Some contractors will do exactly this - a "labor only" or partial scope - and some will not touch a partially finished job. Ask before you start, and be specific about exactly where your work ends and theirs begins.
